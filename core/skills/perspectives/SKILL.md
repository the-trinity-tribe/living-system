---
name: perspectives
description: Look at an important decision from several genuinely different viewpoints, in one session, with evidence kept apart from interpretation and disagreements stated openly; the human makes the one decision. Use when the human says "look at this from different angles", "what would others think", "play devil's advocate" or faces a decision with real consequences.
---

One session, no extra agents. If a plain critical review is enough, say so and do that instead.

1. Write the decision in one sentence and what is known (with sources: files in this workspace, or what the human told you). Mark anything else as unknown.
2. Agree three or four viewpoints with the human that would really disagree, named by role or interest, never by a real person: for example the client, the person doing the work, whoever pays, someone who must live with a mistake. Never invent quotes or speak as a named real person.
3. For each viewpoint, in a few lines: what matters to it, what it would choose, and why. Keep **evidence** (what the files or the human say) apart from **interpretation** (what the viewpoint concludes).
4. One round of replies: where do the viewpoints disagree, and what would change each one's mind? At most two rounds in total.
5. Summarise: where they agree, the real disagreements, what is still unknown, and the options. Do not recommend unless asked.
6. The human decides. If the decision should become a rule, offer to draft a proposal (`AGENTS.md` section 6); never approve it.
