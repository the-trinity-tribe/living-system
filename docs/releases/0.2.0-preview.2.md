# Living System 0.2.0 preview 2

Released 2026-10-24. **A preview for testing and feedback.** The final 0.2.0 follows after a round of hands-on testing.

## In one sentence

Preview 2 makes the agent's room to act explicit and reversible, gives every person their own terms for working with AI, and helps knowledge age gracefully.

## What's new

**Your agent's limits, honestly labelled.** `living.py agency` shows who may do what in your workspace, and marks each line as enforced by a tool or an instruction to the agent.

**Delegation you grant and take back.** Five levels per kind of work: observe, recommend, draft (the default), act with approval, bounded. A delegation is a proposal you approve, with evidence; bounded autonomy needs a scope and a review date within 90 days; publishing, sending, spending, deleting, signing and changing rules never go above "act with approval". You withdraw it by retiring it.

**Your own work agreement.** `me/agreement.md`, in English, German or Polish: how you like answers, when not to interrupt you, which skills to keep, what must never be handed over, when to be challenged, and what time saved is for. The agent reads it and never adds to it.

**Calmer attention.** `park` keeps an idea with what needs deciding and a date to look again; `status` reminds you when it comes. The `tend` skill also tidies knowledge.

**Knowledge that ages well.** Mark how sure a note is (`confidence: low | medium | high`). `living.py compost` moves outdated knowledge to an archive with the date and reason, out of daily use but kept as history.

**Honest evidence.** A `small-experiment` module tries one change and measures it the same way before and after, with business, human, resource and reliability measures kept apart. The evidence summary now reports those four kinds separately. A new [ethics page](../ethics.md) lists every guardrail and whether a tool enforces it.

**See how it fits together.** `living.py atlas` maps which rules are in force and what relies on each, with a diagram. A `perspectives` skill looks at an important decision from several viewpoints, and you decide.

**Docs.** A [command reference](../cli.md), [troubleshooting](../troubleshooting.md), an [architecture overview](../architecture.md) and a [roadmap](../roadmap.md) that explains every deferred or rejected idea. The [pitch page](../../site/index.html) has three new chapters.

## Coming from preview 1

From the Living System folder, after `git pull`:

```sh
python3 tools/living.py upgrade ~/my-workspace
python3 tools/living.py upgrade ~/my-workspace --apply
```

This adds the new agent rules, skills and `me/agreement.md` (only if you have none). Files you edited are kept, with the new version in `.living/upgrade/` to compare. Nothing in `decisions/`, `proposals/`, `projects/` or your existing `me/` files is changed. No file formats changed.

## Known limitations

- Experimental. No login, no identity check, no access control between people; names are labels.
- Delegations, the work agreement, coaching, onboarding and tend are instructions to the agent; they are not yet tested in live sessions. The pitch marks them EXPERIMENTAL.
- The always-approval check for delegations is a keyword check; the agent rule covers what it misses.
- The write guard works in Claude Code, and in Codex once you trust its hooks.
- German and Polish texts still need a native-speaker review.
- Nothing measures time, money or energy saved.

## Quality

Over 400 automated tests on Linux, Windows and macOS with Python 3.10 and 3.13. Seven independent code reviews have found 144 defects; all are fixed, each with a test.
